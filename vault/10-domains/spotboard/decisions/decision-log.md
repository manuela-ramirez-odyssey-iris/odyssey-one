## Decisions

### SPB-01 — "Overflow" renamed to "SpotBoard"
**Decided:** 2026-07-28 (settled in artifact; rename discussed live in the meeting transcript)
**Previous state:** The capability was called **Overflow** / "overflow board" / "overflow quote board". Legacy TMS exposes it as a control literally labelled *overflow*. The PRD is titled "Overflow Bidding PRD v2.0" (07/02/2026).
**Decision:** The product is **SpotBoard** — one word, capital S, capital B, no space. This is the form to use for UI labels, tab names and module nav.
**Rationale:** "Overflow" describes an internal system condition, not what the user is doing. The name change was raised in the meeting by Irina — *"I have to start using the new name or something"* — and Kathleen's wireframe two days later uses `SpotBoard` exclusively, in the page title, the tab, all seven screen headers, the module nav and her own filename.
**Source:** transcript 13:15–13:35 (Irina, Kathleen, Manuela — the rename is discussed mid-sentence and the transcript's own spellings are garbled: *"Botboard"*, *"Spot for"*); wireframe `<title>`, `<h1>`, subtitle *"Overflow Bidding (now 'SpotBoard')"*, and filename `SpotBoard_Wireframes_v1.html`.
**Affects:** All SpotBoard UI labels; vault folder name (`vault/10-domains/spotboard/`); expect "Overflow" to persist in PRD titles, Kathleen's working directory, and legacy TMS. **Watch:** the wireframe's own Screen 1 guardrail still says *"another open bid (Loadboard or Overflow)"* — unresolved whether that is leaked legacy naming or a third channel (see [[../spotboard|spotboard]] §9.1).
> **Appended 2026-07-29 — decision text above is unmodified.** The **Watch** clause is now **resolved and superseded by [[#spb-08--there-are-exactly-two-bidding-channels-the-wireframes-loadboard-or-overflow-guardrail-is-not-a-third|SPB-08]]**: leaked legacy naming, no third channel. The rename decision itself stands in force.

### SPB-02 — Award is not a tender; the awarded spot carrier is appended as the last route-guide row
**Decided:** 2026-07-28 (mechanism explained in the meeting; rendered in the wireframe)
**Previous state:** The Shipments canon recorded only that spot bidding *"loops back to tendering — a formal tender is sent at the bid price for acceptance"* ([[../../shipments/domain-analysis|domain-analysis]] §4). How the awarded carrier appears in the Tender tab was undefined.
**Decision:** Awarding a bid moves the carrier **into** the shipment tendering flow — it does **not** assign the load. The awarded carrier is appended as the **last row of the route guide** on the existing Tender tab, flagged `SPOT RATE` and visually distinct, carrying the quoted amount **plus markup** applied at hand-off. Tendering that row is a separate, subsequent event with its own tender status. If the awarded carrier then declines the tender, the next eligible SpotBoard bid is considered.
**Rationale:** Preserves one carrier-assignment mechanism (the tender) rather than creating a second. Also gives the planner the provenance visibility Kathleen explicitly asked for in the meeting: *"we need visibility in this route guide, in this tender route guide that that was from the quote."*
**Source:** transcript 15:44–16:24 (Irina: *"this carrier will be added as the additional option in your tendering screen… The 5th line will be added for this carrier with the cost he provided… you will be then capable to tender it to that carrier"*) and 16:36 (Kathleen endorses, asks for route-guide visibility); wireframe Screen 4 (*"Award moves the carrier into the shipment tendering flow — it does not assign the load until tendered"*) and Screen 5 (*"Award ≠ tender"*, `SPOT RATE` flag, `$2,690 (bid $2,540 + markup)`).
**Affects:** Tender tab routing-options table (new row source, new flag, markup-applied rate); [[../../shipments/domain-analysis|Shipments domain-analysis]] §3–4; the `Award Carrier & Send to Tender` action.
> **Appended 2026-07-29 — decision text above is unmodified. Re-tested against the PRD: SURVIVES, with one part refined.**
> **Confirmed verbatim by the PRD** — *"Note, tender is a separate event. They can be awarded the load, but they do not get assigned to the load until it is tendered (automatically or manually)"* (PRD, Feature 4); *"move that carrier into the OdysseyONE shipment tendering flow… store awarded quote charges against the shipment record… associate the Quote ID with the awarded shipment"* (Feature 5); *"Markup is applied when the winning carrier is moved from the Overflow board to the tender process… driven by OCM profiles and can either be a percentage or a flat amount"* (Feature 5); and the decline-fallthrough — *"If that awarded carrier declines the tender, then the next best carrier option will be considered for award/notification"* (Feature 4). Feature 7 adds that the planner may instead *"return to the closed quote and award another responding carrier, or may start a new bidding process"* — a human choice, not only an automatic fallthrough.
> **Refined:** the **rendering** is not PRD-sourced. *"Appended as the last row of the route guide"* and the `SPOT RATE` flag appear nowhere in the PRD — they come from Irina (meeting 1, 15:44, *"The 5th line will be added"*) and from the wireframe. The PRD says only that the carrier moves into the tendering flow. The mechanism is firm; the visual treatment remains a proposal.
> **Open, from the PRD:** whether markup is applied automatically post-award or configured per quote by the planner (`OQ-4`, owner Ops/Product). Also note a mild internal tension — Feature 4 says *"Awarded tender will include the quote amount provided on the bid"* while Feature 5 applies markup at that same hand-off; the wireframe reconciles them as `$2,540 bid + markup = $2,690`.
> **Appended 2026-07-29 (v1.3 visual-recovery pass) — decision text above is unmodified. Two corrections to the appended notes, both about markup.**
> **(1) The "Open, from the PRD" note immediately above is WITHDRAWN.** `OQ-4` is **struck through** in the source PDF and was flattened into live text by the MarkItDown conversion — see [[#spb-14--three-of-the-prds-seventeen-open-questions-are-struck-through-the-text-conversion-presented-them-as-live|SPB-14]]. It is a retired question, so it places no qualifier on this decision's *"plus markup"* clause.
> **(2) The mechanism behind *"plus markup"* is now known:** a discrete **`QMU QUOTE MARKUP`** charge line written alongside the carrier's own charges on the awarded cost record, summing into the total that flows to the route-guide row — **[[#spb-15--markup-is-a-discrete-qmu-quote-markup-charge-line-on-the-awarded-carriers-cost-record|SPB-15]]**. This also dissolves the *"mild internal tension"* noted above: Feature 4 and Feature 5 are both literally true, because the tender carries the bid amount *as a line* and the markup *as a separate line*. Nothing in this decision's substance changes; the rendering claim (last row of the route guide, `SPOT RATE` flag) remains a proposal, as the previous note said.

### SPB-03 — SpotBoard is its own tab on the shipment, not a control inside the Tender tab

**Previous state:** Legacy TMS exposes overflow as a **button** on the standard tendering page — *"maybe not additional top from the standard page, they have a button which called overflow"* (Irina).
**Decision:** `SpotBoard` is a peer tab in the shipment tab strip (`Orders · Product · Stops · Tender · Cost Allocation · Instructions · Documents · Notes · SpotBoard`), with three sub-tabs: `Setup & Carriers`, `Live Bids`, `Quote History`.
**Rationale:** Irina's argument, stated in the meeting: the functionality is genuinely different — a different carrier list, a broadcast rather than a sequence, priced responses rather than accept/decline, and a selection step for whom to invite. *"there is a functionality that therefore, that's why it's need to be the separate tab."* Kathleen agreed: *"I could see that happening from another tab that goes back to this screen for who gets awarded."*
**Rationale caveat:** Irina audibly wavered between "button" and "tab" across 12:35–15:05. The wireframe is what settles it. Treat as the working assumption, not a closed question.
**Source:** transcript 12:35–15:05 (Irina), 16:36 (Kathleen); wireframe Screens 1, 4, 5 tab strips and Screen 1 sub-nav.
**Affects:** Shipment tab strip; navigation model; the still-open "Feature 1 launch-point decision" the wireframe flags for David.

### SPB-04 — SpotBoard exists at two altitudes: an in-shipment tab and a standalone cross-shipment module
**Decided:** 2026-07-28
**Previous state:** Legacy overflow was reachable only from inside a single shipment; quote history lived in a separate application entirely (*"Legacy hid history in a separate app"*).
**Decision:** SpotBoard is both. The in-shipment tab handles one shipment's quote ([[../spotboard|Screens 1 and 4]]). A **standalone SpotBoard module** in the top-level nav (`Shipments · Loadboard · SpotBoard · Carriers · Reports`) shows every active and closed spot event across all shipments, with KPI tiles and drill-in to the per-shipment screens, plus a `Quote History` sub-view for search, audit and export — reachable from the module **and** from a shipment.
**Rationale:** Kathleen's own annotation: *"Standalone module, not inside one shipment. Resolves the naming mismatch: a 'board' that shows every spot event."* A board implies a cross-shipment surface; a per-shipment tab alone cannot support the PRD's end-of-day monitoring queues (open bids, closed-unawarded bids). Quote-ID-first search is the operational driver — carriers phone in with only a quote number.
**Source:** wireframe Screens 6 and 7, including both annotation blocks (PRD Feature 10).
**Affects:** Top-level navigation; a new SpotBoard route alongside the existing domain routes; quote-ID search (likely intersects [[../../../20-cross-cutting/global-search/global-search|GlobalSearch]]); placement/permissions still open pending David.

### SPB-05 — Order ID and Load ID are withheld from all carrier-facing surfaces
**Decided:** 2026-07-28
**Previous state:** Not documented for the legacy overflow flow.
**Decision:** Neither the RFQ email nor the carrier bid screen shows Order ID or Load ID. Carriers see the `Quote ID` only. The shipper is shown **by name only** (e.g. "Acme Chemicals"), with no further customer identifiers.
**Rationale:** Stated in bold on the artifact: *"Order ID and Load ID are deliberately excluded to prevent carriers from 'stealing the load.'"* A carrier holding the load identifier could approach the shipper directly and disintermediate Odyssey.
**Source:** wireframe Screen 2 annotation (PRD Features 4 & 11, CE-1) and Screen 3 annotation (PRD Feature 3).
**Affects:** RFQ email template (CE-1); carrier portal lane-detail panel; any future carrier-facing notification. Also drives SPB-04's quote-ID-first search — since carriers can only quote a `QT-` number, search must resolve quote → shipment/load.
> **Appended 2026-07-29 — decision text above is unmodified. Re-tested against the PRD: SURVIVES, unchanged, now with the requirement text behind it.** The wireframe's bolded annotation turns out to be the PRD's own requirement: *"**No IDs will be shared for Order/Shipment to protect from carriers 'stealing the load'**"* (PRD, Feature 4), restated in the email catalog as *"Order/load IDs must be excluded from email"* and *"CE-1 shall include Quote ID, basic load details… **Order ID and Load ID shall be excluded**"* (PRD, Feature 11). Feature 3's carrier-visible field list independently omits both while including `Shipper name`, confirming the name-only treatment. The operational consequence is also PRD-sourced rather than inferred: *"carriers may contact planners with only the quote number and no order or load number… OdysseyONE should support searching by Quote ID to identify the related shipment/load"* (PRD, Feature 10; Appendix B).
> **Appended 2026-07-29 (v1.4) — decision text above is unmodified. Re-tested against the Jana story: SURVIVES, and the story contradicts it at field level.**
> **The threat is real and specific.** Jana's draft puts shipment identifiers into carrier-facing query surfaces, twice: *"filters and sorting based on date, **shipment ID**, bid status, route, etc."* (Jana story, screen summary §4, Bid Listing Page) and *"filters such as **shipment ID**, date range, event type"* (Jana story, screen summary §5, Bid History Page). Her Bid Details Page is also specified as *"**full shipment information**"* with no bounding field list.
> **Why the decision survives anyway.** The PRD names shipment IDs explicitly — *"**No IDs will be shared for Order/Shipment** to protect from carriers 'stealing the load'"* (PRD, Feature 4) — restated in Feature 11, and confirmed visually: the legacy carrier portal's quote list has **no Order or Load column anywhere** (PRD pp.22, 26, read visually). Legacy built an entire internal screen (`MFFQCOFL`) purely so planners could resolve a quote number back to an order/load, *because* the carrier cannot be given the identifier ([[../spotboard|canon]] §14.2). Against that: a **draft** whose screen list is self-described as *"the UI screens the portal would **typically** require."*
> **INFERENCE (short, marked): the `shipment ID` filters are portal boilerplate, not a considered reversal.** The story never mentions load stealing, disintermediation, or SPB-05's concern in any form, so there is no evidence of an intent to overturn it. **Flag it to Jana regardless** — a filter is a query surface, and *"full shipment information"* is precisely the phrase under which withheld identifiers arrive by accident. ### SPB-07 — Loadboard and SpotBoard are separate processes: automated-internal vs planner-initiated-external
**Decided:** 2026-07-29 (stated in a meeting convened specifically to settle the boundary)
**Previous state:** The boundary was an **explicit open question** in the v1 canon. v1 could establish only that Loadboard was a *sibling* of SpotBoard — from Kathleen's module nav listing them as peers and Jana's status machine running them as two sequential states — and recorded, on the evidence then available, *"No evidence in either artifact for 'lowboard is mostly automations.'"* What Loadboard actually **was** remained undocumented, and v1 said so: *"Loadboard is out of scope for this canon — we have no artifact describing it."*
**Decision:** Loadboard and SpotBoard are **two distinct escalation channels**, differing on every axis that matters:
- **Loadboard** is an **automated** process that posts the shipment to Odyssey's **internal brokerage desks** — a truckload desk in **Kennesaw** and a separate **bulk brokerage group** — for them to claim or ignore. It keeps the work inside Odyssey.
- **SpotBoard** is a **planner's decision** that invites **external carriers** to submit a **price**. It moves the work outside Odyssey.

Loadboard is conventionally **earlier** in the lifecycle, but that ordering is **explicitly not enforced**. Neither feeds the other: they share an upstream condition and are coupled only by runtime mutual exclusion (SPB-08). Loadboard is **out of scope for the SpotBoard canon** except as boundary material.
**Rationale:** The distinction is functional, not cosmetic — automated vs human-initiated, insourced vs outsourced, claim vs priced bid. Conflating them would put an automated internal-brokerage posting behind a planner-facing bid-composition UI, which is the wrong surface for it. It also **corrects a v1 false negative**: the original verbal hypothesis that "lowboard is mostly automations" was right on substance and wrong only on the word — Loadboard *is* the automated one. v1's rejection of the term *"lowboard"* stands (a mishearing of "load board"); v1's rejection of the automation claim does not, and was an evidence gap rather than a misreading.
**Source:** `Carrier Load Board vs Overflow Board meeting.txt`. Kathleen O'Donnell, 0:45 — *"they're separate processes. And overflow board is a decision that's made by the planner, whereas the load board is an automated process to take the shipment. The load board is actually just going to our internal load board"*; 1:04 — *"we have a brokerage team in Kennesaw that will get all shipments that are, say, like truckload kind of shipments. And then we have a bulk brokerage group that will get bulk shipments. So the load board is only going to send those shipments to them for them to action if they want them or not"*; 1:26 — *"earlier in the process, right, Doug, than overflow, if it's going to happen at all"*. `Speaker 1` (INFERENCE: Doug), 1:40 — *"in theory, load board is before overflow; doesn't have to be, but… In theory."* Question posed by Saikat Ghosh (Cognizant), 0:34.
Corroborating, pre-existing: wireframe Screens 6–7 module nav (peers); `vault/10-domains/shipments/grooming/0401-jana.vtt` 15:57 (two sequential states); `Loadboard Expiry` column in [[../../shipments/domain-analysis|domain-analysis]] §3.
> **Appended 2026-07-29 — decision text above is unmodified. Re-tested against the PRD: SURVIVES.** The PRD predates this decision's source meeting by 27 days, so the meeting outranks it on this boundary — but there is nothing to arbitrate, and the PRD corroborates from a third direction. It treats Loadboard and SpotBoard as **peer destinations of the same upstream failure**: *"Should OdysseyONE use OCM profiles, master data settings, or both to determine whether a shipment moves from failed tendering to **load board, overflow, or user review**?"* (PRD, §8 `OQ-5`) — three alternatives, in a document that never discusses the comparison. It also makes the runtime lock **reciprocal**, which the wireframe had dropped: *"There also cannot be another open bid (Loadboard or Overflow) for that shipment. **The system cannot tender when shipment is in Overflow or Loadboard.**"* (PRD, Feature 1). The PRD says **nothing** about how a shipment reaches the Loadboard — an absence, not a conflict, and the same gap Doug's uncaptured answer still leaves open.
> **Appended 2026-07-29 (v1.3 visual-recovery pass) — decision unchanged, one citation qualified.** The `OQ-5` row cited just above is **struck through in the source PDF** and was flattened into live text by the conversion ([[#spb-14--three-of-the-prds-seventeen-open-questions-are-struck-through-the-text-conversion-presented-them-as-live|SPB-14]]). **This does not weaken the corroboration.** The citation was never to the question's *open status* — it was to its *framing*, that a document which nowhere discusses the Loadboard/SpotBoard comparison nonetheless lists load board, overflow and user review as three peer outcomes of failed tendering. That sentence is still on the page and still frames them as peers. What changes is only downstream bookkeeping: canon §4's note that *"the configuration source is still open per `OQ-5`"* should no longer cite an open question.
**Affects:** [[../spotboard|spotboard]] §2 (lifecycle diagram now shows the Loadboard leg), §4 (rewritten and retitled *"Loadboard vs SpotBoard"*), §7 (vocabulary), §10, §11. **Supersedes** the v1 §4 finding *"No evidence… for 'lowboard is mostly automations'"*. Establishes that a `vault/10-domains/loadboard/` folder is **justified in principle but not yet warranted** — trigger conditions in [[../spotboard|spotboard]] §4. Does **not** affect [[#spb-02--award-is-not-a-tender-the-awarded-spot-carrier-is-appended-as-the-last-route-guide-row|SPB-02]] or [[#spb-03--spotboard-is-its-own-tab-on-the-shipment-not-a-control-inside-the-tender-tab|SPB-03]], which this transcript does not touch.

### SPB-08 — There are exactly two bidding channels; the wireframe's "Loadboard or Overflow" guardrail is not a third

**Previous state:** [[#spb-01--overflow-renamed-to-spotboard|SPB-01]] carried an explicit **Watch** clause: *"the wireframe's own Screen 1 guardrail still says 'another open bid (Loadboard or Overflow)' — unresolved whether that is leaked legacy naming or a third channel."* v1 §9.1 recorded the same as an open conflict, guessing at leaked naming but unable to prove it. The stakes were real: a third channel would have meant a whole undocumented surface.
**Decision:** **There is no third channel.** The guardrail reads: *a SpotBoard quote may not be opened when there is an active/accepted tender, an open Loadboard posting, or an already-open SpotBoard quote.* "Overflow" in that sentence is **stale copy for SpotBoard itself** — the pre-rename name (SPB-01) surviving in the guardrail text. The copy should be corrected to say "SpotBoard".
**Rationale:** The loadboard meeting was convened expressly to enumerate and distinguish the boards — Saikat: *"we like to know what is the difference between load board and overflow board"* — and it enumerates exactly two. Kathleen answers with a two-item contrast and no one, at any point, names a third. A channel that goes unmentioned in the one meeting held to enumerate channels is very unlikely to exist.
**Inference marker:** the transcript was never asked about the guardrail sentence. This applies the two-board finding to it. Short inference; the competing reading now has **zero** supporting evidence, where at v1 it had ambiguity in its favour.
**Source:** `Carrier Load Board vs Overflow Board meeting.txt`, 0:34 (Saikat Ghosh, framing the question as a two-way comparison) and 0:45 (Kathleen O'Donnell, *"they're separate processes"*, contrasting two and only two). Guardrail text: wireframe, Screen 1 annotation.
**Affects:** **Supersedes the Watch clause on [[#spb-01--overflow-renamed-to-spotboard|SPB-01]]** — that decision's substance (the rename) is unchanged and remains in force; only its open caveat is now closed. Closes [[../spotboard|spotboard]] §9.1. Wireframe Screen 1 guardrail copy needs a wording fix. Confirms the SpotBoard entry guardrail has exactly three blocking conditions, not four.

In a document dated 07/02/2026 that names this product *Overflow* on every page and the other one *load board*, `(Loadboard or Overflow)` can only mean *the Loadboard, or this product*. The competing reading — a third, unmentioned channel — is now excluded by the source itself rather than by argument from the meeting.

### SPB-09 — Carrier access for v1 is a per-recipient tokenized email link; the logged-in carrier area is deferred until user management exists
**Decided:** 2026-07-29 (Kathleen's written proposal; formally still `OQ-1`'s owner's to ratify)
**Previous state:** Genuinely undecided, and known to be. The PRD put two options on the table and refused to choose — **Option A, Net Native user management** (*"Today, the TMS carrier portal users are provisioned through Net Native. This provides managed credentials but requires coordination with the Net Native team for each carrier onboarding"*) versus **Option B, secure token-based access** (*"Each RFQ email to a carrier contains a unique, time-limited signed token. The carrier clicks the link and is taken directly to their quote form without a login. Token expires when the quote closes. No user account required"*) — under the heading *"🔒 Carrier Authentication — Decision Required"*, with **`Recommendation: TBD`** and **`ACTION: Dev/Thomas to confirm preferred approach before development begins`** (PRD, §3; `OQ-1`, owner Thomas). The v1.1 canon could record only the wireframe's hedge: *"token link (Net Native credentials or signed token — OQ in Section 3)."*
**Decision:** **Option B for v1.** A carrier receives the `CE-1` RFQ email containing a link carrying **a token scoped to that recipient**, which opens the single-quote response screen directly. **There is no carrier login in v1.** Token expiry is bounded by the PRD's own non-functional — *"Signed carrier access tokens must expire no later than quote close time"* (PRD, §6).

**Deferred until user management exists**, as a named future state rather than an unknown: a logged-in area where a carrier user sees **all their quotes, the time remaining on the current one, and historical quotes** (Kathleen note). **INFERENCE, short and marked:** that description maps onto PRD Feature 3's logged-in requirements — *"display all open quote requests available to the authenticated carrier"*, *"a Quote Requests view for open/active bids"*, *"a Quote History view for closed requests"*, *"export of quote history data"* — so those are the Feature 3 bullets that do not ship in v1. Kathleen does not enumerate them herself; the mapping is ours.

**The inherited limitation, recorded because it shapes the deferred state:** isolation is at the **carrier-organization** boundary, never the user boundary. *"The way that Irina built tracking is that any carrier can login and see all tracking for their carrier. This will be the same for the carrier quote. ### SPB-12 — SpotBoard entry is not gated on route-guide exhaustion, and the tender lock is reciprocal

2. *"There also cannot be another open bid (Loadboard or Overflow) for that shipment."*

(PRD, Feature 1, "UI Constraints".) Legacy enforces the same coupling harder: *"before send/modify/close, an active tender on the load hard-stops the action"* (PRD, Appendix A.7).

### SPB-13 — Legacy carriers choose pickup/delivery dates *within a planner-set window*; they do not propose dates
**Decided:** 2026-07-29 (recognised on reading the page image; the underlying legacy behavior is long-standing)
**Previous state:** A recorded, unresolved contradiction inside the PRD — [[../spotboard|spotboard]] §9.8 at v1.2. Feature 3's legacy note says carriers **cannot** propose dates: *"The legacy MFFCOFL workflow does not support carriers proposing alternate pickup/delivery dates — dates are planned by the system/planner and presented to the carrier. It was also listed as an open item in canon §10.
**Decision:** **Both statements are true and there is no contradiction.** The legacy carrier bid form contains a **`Flexible Dates`** panel in which `Pickup` and `Delivery` are editable **but bounded by a read-only `Earliest` and `Latest`** set upstream by the planner. The carrier **selects a date inside a window**; it cannot propose a date outside one, and cannot originate a date where no window was configured. That is the **`Flexible Pickup` / `Flexible Delivery`** feature — the one with 23 active configurations each — rendered from the carrier's side. Feature 3 is correct that *proposal* is net-new; Appendix B is correct that dates are *editable*.

**Consequences, kept separate:**
1. **`OQ-6` is a genuine net-new-capability question** and stays open on its original terms. v1.2's worry that it was already answered is dismissed.
2. **The wireframe's Screen 3 is missing an affordance.** It lists no date fields in the carrier bid form at all, while legacy gives the carrier two editable dates whenever flexibility is configured. Not drift — a coverage gap, and one that matters because Feature 1 requires flexible pickup to carry forward as-is.
3. The canon's existing rule *"carriers see dates, planners see the window not the day count"* is confirmed and made concrete: what the carrier sees is **an Earliest/Latest pair plus an editable value**, not a day count.
**Rationale:** The distinction between *choosing within a bound* and *proposing a value* is the whole of `OQ-6`, and the PRD's two passages were describing the two halves of it from different vantage points. Leaving the conflict recorded as unresolved would have kept a design question open that the evidence answers, and worse, risked a v1.2 reader concluding that carrier-proposed dates already exist and can simply be carried forward.
**Source:** **PRD p.23, read visually** — the `Quote Entry` modal's `Flexible Dates` panel: *"\*Choose pickup and delivery dates:"* over `Pickup` `08-Jul-2026` with `Earliest` `06-Jul-2026` / `Latest` `10-Jul-2026`, and `Delivery` the same shape, format `(dd-Mon-yyyy)`. **Affects:** **Closes [[../spotboard|spotboard]] §9.8** (rewritten from conflict to resolution) and the matching §10 open item. Refines `OQ-6`'s standing in §10's PRD open-questions table. ### SPB-15 — Markup is a discrete `QMU QUOTE MARKUP` charge line on the awarded carrier's cost record
**Decided:** 2026-07-29 (recognised on reading the page image; the legacy behavior is long-standing)
**Previous state:** [[#spb-02--award-is-not-a-tender-the-awarded-spot-carrier-is-appended-as-the-last-route-guide-row|SPB-02]] recorded that the awarded carrier is handed to Tender *"carrying the quoted amount **plus markup** applied at hand-off"*, and canon §6 recorded the timing rule verbatim from Feature 5 — *"Markup is applied when the winning carrier is moved from the Overflow board to the tender process… driven by OCM profiles and can either be a percentage or a flat amount."* **The mechanism was unknown.** The only concrete rendering in evidence was the wireframe's summary string `$2,690 (bid $2,540 + markup)`, which reads as an opaque uplift on a single number, and the canon flagged the whole account as qualified by the (as it turns out, retired) `OQ-4`.
**Decision:** **Markup is materialised as an itemised charge line, not as an adjustment to the bid.** On the awarded carrier's cost record on the tender side, the carrier's submitted charges and the Odyssey markup sit as sibling rows summing to a total:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base Cost | `$100.000 USD` |
| `FUE` FUEL | `$53.430` |
| `HAZ` HAZMAT | `$100.000` |
| `TIP` SERVER TIPS | `$10.000` |
| **`QMU` QUOTE MARKUP** | **`$50.000`** |
| **Total Cost** | **`$313.430 USD`** |

and that `313.430 USD` is the figure carried on the route-guide row in the tender grid. The record is stamped `Quote Type = Carrier Overflow` and `Quote Status = Completed`, and is editable (`New` / `Edit` / `Delete` on the charge grid, `Edit Quote` on the header).

**Three consequences:**
1. **The bid stays legible after markup.** A planner looking at the tender row can decompose it into what the carrier quoted and what Odyssey added, because they are separate rows with separate codes. The wireframe's single string `$2,690 (bid $2,540 + markup)` conveys the same fact but as prose; the data model behind it is a charge list.
2. **Markup is opt-out per profile.** The `COFL Markup` OCM profile's own description reads *"Use this profile to define overflow quote markup fees. **NO means do not apply**…"* — so a configuration exists in which no `QMU` line is written at all.
3. **`QMU` joins the charge-code vocabulary** alongside the OCM-configured accessorials (`HZC`, `TKM`, `TAR`, `HT`, `MSC` on one profile; `Hazmat` / `Pickup` / `Tips` / `Tolls` on another). It is an Odyssey-side code, not a carrier-quotable one — carriers never see it, since it is applied after award.
**Rationale:** This is the difference between a display rule and a data model, and it lands on both the Tender tab and cost allocation. A markup implemented as an uplift produces one number and loses the split; a markup implemented as a charge line preserves the audit trail the PRD's own auditability non-functional demands (*"every response, closure reason and award decision logged and traceable"*), and it is what the system being replaced already does. Recording it now avoids OdysseyONE reinventing the cheaper version and then needing the split back for cost allocation. **This does not decide OdysseyONE's implementation** — it establishes the legacy baseline and what carrying it forward would mean.

### SPB-17 — Non-participation is first-class: "No Bid Submitted" is a status, and no explicit decline is required
**Decided:** 2026-07-29 — **proposed**, in a draft, and recorded as **the first named treatment of non-participation** rather than a ratified label. The *concept* is well-founded and uncontradicted by anything in evidence; the *exact string* is Jana's proposal, and she hedges it herself — *"'No Bid Submitted' **or equivalent** non-participation status"*. **Deliberately separated from [[#spb-16--carrier-access-is-contested-between-two-pms-kathleens-tokenized-link-and-janas-authenticated-portal-nothing-is-decided|SPB-16]]: this holds whichever access model wins.**
**Previous state:** A carrier that received an RFQ and never answered had **no representation in any persisted vocabulary**, and the canon carried five status sets without noticing the hole. In the **legacy** carrier grid the row stays at **`Sent`** forever (PRD pp.21–22, 25, 29, read visually) — a terminal outcome stored as an in-flight status, where `Sent` at minute 1 and `Sent` at minute 121 are the same row. In the **wireframe** it is an **em-dash `—`** (wireframe, Screen 4): a rendering of absence, not a value, with nothing to persist, filter, count or audit. The **PRD's outcome set** (`Awarded` / `Not awarded` / `Cancelled` / `Manual intervention` / …, Feature 10) is **quote-level** and therefore the wrong altitude. Note the asymmetry: **`Declined` is persisted, timestamped and reversible** (*"status would move out of Declined"*, PRD Feature 3) while its complement — silence — was not stored at all.
**Decision:** **Non-participation is a first-class, named, per-carrier outcome, and it is not a decline.** Four rules, all from the artifact:
1. **`No Bid Submitted` is a status**, carried per carrier in the bid summary — *"Odyssey One must reflect the carrier as having 'No Bid Submitted' or equivalent non-participation status in the bid summary"* (Jana story, AC "Status Visibility in Odyssey One"). Assigned **at window close** (*"Given the bid window has closed, When no bid has been received from a specific carrier"*) and terminal.
2. **No explicit decline is required.** *"Carrier participation is voluntary. The portal must not require an explicit action to decline participation; lack of bid submission by the deadline is considered non-participation."* (Business Rules "No Mandatory Carrier Response").
3. **Expiry is the non-participation event** — *"the event naturally expires for them without requiring any explicit decline"* (Business Rules "Bid Window Enforcement"); *"the bid opportunity for that carrier expires automatically with no further action from the carrier"* (AC "Bid Expiry for Non-Participating Carriers").
4. **It must not read as a decision.** *"Non-participation events appear in history simply as 'No Bid Submitted' or similar wording, **without implying a formal decision from the carrier**."* (Business Rules "History"). **This is the semantic constraint and the reason `Declined` cannot be reused** — `Declined` asserts an act the carrier never performed.

Plus an audit requirement — *"Odyssey One must record and display carriers who did not submit bids within the bid timeframe, to support a complete audit trail and enable user decision-making"* (Business Rules "Odyssey One Visibility") — and a **carrier-side** expiry state: *"if no bid is submitted by deadline → screen reflects **'Bid Expired'**"* (screen summary §4), with history showing *"shipments where the carrier submitted a bid as well as events they simply did not participate in (bid expired)"* (AC "Carrier Portal History").

**It EXTENDS rather than conflicts — with one misreading that must be avoided.** Rule 2 says the portal must not *require* a decline; it does **not** say `Decline` is removed. Read as *"remove the Decline action"* it would contradict PRD Feature 3's `Submit Bid` / `Update Bid` / `Decline` with re-bid explicitly permitted, the legacy `Decline` / `Submit` button pair (PRD p.23, read visually), and the persisted `Declined` status. **Read as written — decline stays offered, silence is not a failure state — it is a clean extension.** The model therefore carries **two distinct non-award outcomes that must never be collapsed**: an act (`Declined`, carrier-written, reversible while open) and an absence (`No Bid Submitted`, close-written, terminal).
**Rationale:** Three reasons this is worth a decision entry rather than a canon footnote. **(1) It fills a hole the canon did not know it had** — five documented status vocabularies and not one of them can express the most common carrier outcome in a broadcast auction. **(2) It changes who writes a status.** Every other value in the legacy carrier-row set is written by a transmission or a carrier response; this one is written by **the clock**, at close. That is a new writer in the model, and it is the entry's only structural consequence ([[../data/quote-model|data/quote-model]] §3.4). **(3) It is the durable part of a contested artifact.** The Jana story will be read primarily as one side of an authentication argument; filing it that way would discard its best content. Non-participation is orthogonal to that argument and would be needed even if Kathleen's model wins outright — a tokenized-link carrier can ignore the link exactly as easily as a logged-in one can.
**Scope limits, stated plainly.** This does **not** decide: where `No Bid Submitted` surfaces (planner bid table, monitoring board, quote history, carrier history — unspecified everywhere); whether partial non-participation warrants any notification (**`IE-1` is quote-level**, firing only on *"zero bids"* — Jana's requirement is per-carrier and applies even when others bid, so this is a **gap, not a conflict**); or the final string. It also does **not** close the PRD's adjacent unnumbered question, *"Should we add another quote status for carrier history so quotes don't stay in review status?"* (PRD, Feature 7) — that asks about **quotes stuck in review**, this is about **carriers who never answered**. Decidable together; not the same question.
